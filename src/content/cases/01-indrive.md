We spent several months working on a single project. The final text was not supposed to exceed ten pages, but in practice, it turned out to be one of the most challenging projects we had ever worked on. And certainly the most carefully thought through. The project was a culture handbook for inDrive employees.

The challenge was that the client wanted a genuinely meaningful document. It had to describe, briefly and clearly, a unique company with many distinctive features — a company with a truly distinctive mission and driving force, bringing together people from very different countries and cultures, and operating in a competitive market. And it had to do so without alienating people who had worked there for years, while helping newcomers understand how everything worked. It also needed to remain relevant for a reasonably long time, despite the constantly changing environment.

To write those ten pages, we first conducted twelve in-depth interviews and analysed an enormous amount of material — documents, policies, interviews and podcasts. We also watched a film and read a book. Then we put together a draft and ran three focus groups with indriveans from different countries, doing completely different jobs. We wanted to make sure we had truly captured the spirit of the company, included all the important values, got everything right and left nothing out. inDrive moves very fast, and some parts were becoming outdated even as we were writing them. So we made the handbook flexible and resilient, allowing significant changes to be easily incorporated.

The project also had a second phase. After writing the full version of the text — which we called the master text — we came up with several lighter, more playful formats. For example, we created a quiz based on the handbook, explained the values through GIFs and developed ideas for posters.

Dozens of people took part in the project — joining interviews and focus groups, gathering materials, approving quotes, discussing ideas and offering advice. This is incredibly important for a document like this: it now genuinely represents many of the company's employees.
